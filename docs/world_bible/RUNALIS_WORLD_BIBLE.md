## Canon governance

This bible defines immutable foundations. Future content may reveal that a priest, kingdom, book, or spirit is mistaken. It may not silently change objective canon.

### Canon layers

1. **Objective truth:** facts stated in this bible outside an explicitly attributed belief.
2. **Divine memory:** what a God or ancient Primordial remembers. This can be biased, incomplete, or altered by the Veiling.
3. **Institutional history:** what temples, kingdoms, and the Covenant teach.
4. **Folk history:** regional stories, songs, taboos, and practical superstition.
5. **Player knowledge:** evidence the player has personally encountered. This should rarely equal the whole objective truth.

The player should encounter layers three through five naturally. Objective truth is a development tool, not an excuse for exposition.

### Immutable pillars

- Reality is sustained by nine necessary principles, although only Eight Gods remain publicly known.
- The Gods are indispensable but neither omnipotent nor morally perfect.
- The forgotten Ninth governed selfhood, refusal, contradiction, and unrealized possibility.
- The Divine War was a conflict over how much freedom reality could survive, not good against evil.
- The Eight won, erased the Ninth from accessible history, and preserved existence through a foundational lie.
- Primordials are divine persons with agency, politics, and history—not angels or demons.
- Primearch is an office of delegated divine authority, not merely a measure of strength.
- Seraphel is a Primearch of Avarr. Malakar is a devastatingly powerful Primordial of Varka's lineage, but never held Primearch authority.
- Faith creates access, interpretation, and stability. It does not create Gods from nothing.
- All lasting power demands a proportionate cost or displacement.
- Mortal selfhood cannot be made perfectly obedient. The reason is the deepest surviving trace of the Ninth.

### Discovery rule

Every canonical fact intended for players must have at least two non-expository discovery channels. Preferred channels are architecture, ritual practice, enemy behavior, relic function, landscape change, item provenance, biased records, and consequences. Direct explanation should confirm or contest evidence the player has already seen.

# PART 1 – Cosmology

## 1.1 Before the world: the Unbounded

Before place, duration, matter, or identity, there was the **Unbounded**: not an empty void, but limitless possibility without stable difference. Nothing could persist because nothing was required to remain itself from one instant to the next.

Nine enduring intelligences emerged when possibility first resisted changing. Later civilizations call them Gods, but they were initially **Accords**—self-aware principles by which reality could remain coherent.

The Nine did not create existence from nothing. They made existence capable of continuing.

Each Accord stabilized a necessary relationship:

- testimony made events retain meaning;
- change allowed continuity without stagnation;
- death allowed endings and inheritance;
- reciprocity allowed life to consume without consuming everything;
- measure allowed matter and craft to hold shape;
- conflict allowed limits to be tested and defended;
- exchange allowed separate beings to cooperate;
- imagination allowed minds to perceive what was absent;
- refusal allowed a being to remain itself against every outside claim.

The last principle was the Ninth.

This origin strengthens Runalis because creation is a negotiated stability rather than a single benevolent act. Every civilization depends upon all the principles, including those it fears.

## 1.2 The making of Runalis

The Nine drew a bounded continuity from the Unbounded. They called the act **the First Keeping**. Mortal languages later used **Runalis** for the physical world produced by that Keeping.

Runalis has three inseparable components:

1. **The Physical Realm:** matter, time, embodied life, distance, scarcity, and irreversible consequence.
2. **The Divine Realms:** eight surviving domains where one Accord is overwhelmingly strong and the others remain present only by treaty.
3. **The Interstice:** the pressure between realms, containing unclaimed memories, failed manifestations, broken routes, and scars left by the Ninth's removal.

The world is not a planet surrounded by eight distant heavens. It is the shared boundary where the divine principles limit one another enough for mortal life to exist.

## 1.3 The Physical Realm

The Physical Realm is uniquely important because no God has sole jurisdiction there. A stone contains Kharum's measure, Maereth's capacity for weathering and growth, Orun's promise of eventual ending, and Eidren's trace of refusal. A promise invokes Avarr, but the speaker can still break it. A road invokes Ilyra, but travelers can turn aside.

This mixture produces mortality and freedom. It also makes the Physical Realm the only place where genuinely unprecedented choices can occur.

The Gods value mortal civilization for different reasons:

- it demonstrates whether their philosophies work under limitation;
- it produces forms of culture impossible in a single-principle realm;
- it anchors routes through which Divine Realms remain connected;
- it generates faith, which gives divine powers precise shapes and names;
- it can create solutions the Gods themselves cannot conceive.

## 1.4 The Divine Realms

A Divine Realm is not an afterlife reserved for good followers. It is a reality in which one God's principle approaches completeness.

The realms have geography, inhabitants, resources, dangers, institutions, and borders, but mortal descriptions are approximations. A traveler may perceive Avarr's realm as an endless city of courts and public fires because the mortal mind requires streets and buildings to understand witnessed order.

The Eight surviving realms are joined through ancient **Concordant Ways**. Most were damaged during the Divine War. Ruins, shrines, bloodlines, and relics can become temporary thresholds where a Way touches Runalis.

Souls do not automatically belong to a patron God. At death, a person's accumulated affinities, obligations, ancestry, choices, and burial rites pull their remaining Essence toward a realm. Some Essence remains in the Physical Realm as memory, inheritance, haunting, or material fertility. Some is lost in the Interstice.

No mortal religion possesses a complete map of this process.

## 1.5 Gods and reality

A God is simultaneously:

- a person with preferences, attachments, errors, and memory;
- the greatest living expression of a necessary principle;
- the sovereign intelligence of a Divine Realm;
- a participant in treaties that keep the Physical Realm stable.

Gods cannot freely rewrite reality. They can intensify their own principle, bargain with another, delegate power, or exploit an existing threshold. A God attempting full physical manifestation would force the surrounding world to obey that God's principle too completely. Avarr would turn every ambiguity into binding testimony. Orun would make every ending final. Maereth would dissolve cities into perfect cycles of consumption and regrowth.

Divine limitation is therefore structural, not benevolent restraint.

## 1.6 The forgotten Ninth: Eidren, the Unwritten

The Ninth God's canonical name is **Eidren**. The name must not become ordinary public knowledge before late game.

Eidren governed:

- individual selfhood;
- refusal and consent;
- contradiction;
- unrealized possibility;
- departure from assigned purpose;
- the right of a created being to become something its creator did not intend.

Eidren's symbol was an open space interrupting a completed circle. No surviving public faith recognizes it as a divine sign. It persists unconsciously in broken wedding rings, doors deliberately left unlatched, the empty ninth position in ancient foundations, and legal customs allowing a final refusal.

Eidren's Divine Realm was **The Unwritten Country**, a place whose geography changed whenever it was conclusively mapped. It contained every road not taken, but it could not preserve all of them without strain.

Eidren was neither a liberator without fault nor an imprisoned evil. Eidren believed no created purpose could remain morally binding without continuing consent. This made possible moral growth, rebellion against tyranny, invention, apostasy, and personal love. It also allowed Primordials to abandon necessary offices, weapons to refuse their wielders, plagues to exceed designed limits, and contradictory realities to compete for existence.

Eidren's fatal error was treating every limitation as potentially illegitimate. During the Divine War, Eidren opened too many unrealized possibilities at once. Regions existed in several incompatible histories. Dead armies returned with memories of victories that had never occurred. Children inherited mutually exclusive bloodlines. Reality began losing the ability to decide what had happened.

The Eight concluded that the world could not survive an unbounded Ninth. Eidren concluded that a perfectly stable world would become a prison whose inmates could no longer recognize captivity.

Both were correct.

## 1.7 The Divine War

The conflict now called the Divine War began as a series of disputes over created beings who refused their assigned functions. Eidren offered them sanctuary. Several Primearchs argued that divine purpose could evolve. Others saw abandoned offices causing famine, unburied dead, failed seasons, and collapsing boundaries.

The war divided every realm. It was never simply Eight against One:

- some of Eidren's Primordials imposed “freedom” by destroying every institution capable of limiting them;
- some servants of the Eight protected mortal communities from reality fractures;
- some Primearchs defected from their Gods;
- mortal kingdoms exploited divine chaos for conquest;
- later atrocities were falsely attributed to whichever side lost local control.

The war reshaped existence in four lasting ways:

1. The Concordant Ways shattered, leaving divine ruins and unstable thresholds across Runalis.
2. Primordial Essence entered mortal bloodlines, creating hereditary transformations and impossible ancestries.
3. Divine weapons and realm-machines were stranded in the Physical Realm.
4. History itself became unreliable where competing possibilities had overlapped.

## 1.8 The Veiling of Eidren

The Eight did not kill Eidren. A necessary Accord cannot be destroyed without destroying the relationship it sustains.

Instead, they performed **the Veiling**:

- Eidren's name was removed from the Concordant treaties.
- The Unwritten Country was cut away from stable routes.
- Eidren's greatest Primordials were killed, bound, divided, or induced to forget their origin.
- public symbols, calendars, genealogies, and temple plans were rewritten over generations;
- the principle of refusal was dispersed into mortal selfhood so no single divine sovereign could wield it completely.

The Eight accepted a terrible compromise. Every mortal would retain a fragment of refusal, but the God who explained and defended that principle would vanish from accessible history.

The official histories claim there were always Eight. The lie persists because remembering Eidren is not merely intellectual. A sufficiently accurate name, symbol, and account can reopen a route to the Unwritten Country.

The Veiling ended the worst fractures. It also created the Interstice, orphaned bloodlines, and a permanent weakness in the divine order.

## 1.9 Metaphysical laws

These laws govern all magic, relics, bloodlines, divine encounters, and supernatural stories.

### The Law of Cost

Power cannot create a lasting change without moving, consuming, binding, exposing, or sacrificing something proportionate. Costs may be delayed or displaced, but never absent. “Free” miracles are evidence that someone else is paying.

### The Law of Correspondence

Like patterns create routes between realms: oath and flame for Avarr, tide and grief for Thessa, names and burial for Orun. Symbols do not work because they are decorative passwords; they reproduce a divine relationship in miniature.

### The Law of Thresholds

Divine influence requires a threshold: a shrine, vessel, bloodline, relic, repeated rite, aligned landscape, willing host, or wound in reality. Stronger manifestation requires a more exact and costly threshold.

### The Law of Dominion

No God can fully enter the Physical Realm without overwriting its mixed nature. Primearchs can manifest more safely because they carry a bounded portion of authority. Ordinary Primordials require narrower anchors.

### The Law of Names

A true name records relationships, history, obligations, and recognized identity. Knowing it grants leverage, not absolute command. Erasing a name breaks routes and obligations but leaves a scar that careful observers can detect.

### The Law of Witness

Shared observation stabilizes one version of an event. Testimony, monuments, records, and communal rituals can strengthen reality after divine disturbance. Propaganda can therefore become metaphysically dangerous without making popular belief objectively true.

### The Law of Echoes

Repeated acts mark places and objects. A battlefield remembers fear; a courthouse remembers judgment; a tool remembers its work. Echoes explain hauntings, legendary weapons, responsive ruins, and why archaeology is dangerous.

### The Law of Continuity

Essence changes state but is not casually annihilated. Death distributes a being's Essence among realm affinity, descendants, place, memory, and the Interstice. Reassembly is possible but never restores a person without alteration and cost.

### The Law of Refusal

No self-aware soul can be made perfectly obedient forever. Coercion, rewriting, possession, and purification can suppress refusal but produce fractures, residues, or delayed resistance. This law is Eidren's surviving legacy and the hidden reason Zephyr's manufacture requires consent, dependency, and manipulated hope.

### The Law of Divine Distance

The longer a Primordial remains embodied in Runalis, the more it must become subject to time, hunger, injury, and local identity. Remaining indefinitely means becoming less divine and more mortal—or deforming the surrounding region into an extension of its Realm.

## 1.10 Natural player discovery

Players should infer cosmology through:

- eight public niches surrounding a sealed or repurposed ninth foundation;
- relics that demand visible costs despite temple claims of free blessing;
- the same event recorded differently in stone, song, and spirit echo;
- Primordials weakened by long embodiment or transforming their surroundings;
- bloodline items containing incompatible genealogies;
- architecture whose measurements repeatedly leave an unexplained interval;
- Covenant purification that suppresses identity but cannot remove a final private habit;
- maps that become inaccurate near remnants of the Unwritten Country.

No early book should state, “There was a Ninth God.” Early evidence should establish that the number Eight does not fully explain the world.

# PART 2 – The Eight Gods

## 2.1 Avarr, the Witnessed Flame

- **Domains:** testimony, oaths, civic legitimacy, public memory, responsibility, protective light.
- **Personality:** patient, exacting, deeply attentive, and dangerously convinced that accountability requires visibility.
- **Symbol:** a vertical flame enclosed by two open hands.
- **Philosophy:** power is legitimate only when witnessed, named, and answerable. A private promise is weaker than one made before those who bear its consequences.
- **Divine Realm:** **The City of Open Courts**, an immense inhabited city where every deed casts a visible light and every office has a public seat.
- **Relationships:** respects Kharum's standards and Ilyra's contracts; frequently conflicts with Nymbr over necessary secrecy and with Varka over emergency authority. Loved Eidren's defense of consent but feared unaccountable refusal.
- **Divine War:** initially sought arbitration. After contradictory histories destroyed the reliability of testimony, Avarr became an architect of the Veiling. Avarr still regards this as the greatest unconfessed act in creation.
- **Modern worship:** magistrates, wardens, healers, soldiers, witnesses, marriage houses, and the Covenant. Public lamps are lit when civic decisions are announced.
- **Civilizational influence:** courts, written contracts, offices, public archives, oaths of service, sanctuary law, and the idea that rulers must justify command.
- **Patron races:** humans were Avarr's first direct mortal people, designed for social flexibility and shared institutions rather than a fixed ecological role. Many human cultures dispute Avarr's ownership of that origin.
- **Visual identity:** warm ivory stone, smoke-dark bronze, amber glass, open plazas, visible structural supports, paired hands, and light directed outward rather than upward.

**Why it strengthens the game:** Avarr makes “holy light” politically specific. Light can protect, reveal, interrogate, surveil, and shame. The Covenant can perform genuine miracles while weaponizing visibility.

**Natural discovery:** courthouse foundations beneath chapels; lamps that brighten when a speaker believes their own statement but do not prove it true; shields engraved with the names of those they failed to protect.

## 2.2 Thessa, the Returning Tide

- **Domains:** change, migration, water, grief, adaptation, seasonal return.
- **Personality:** compassionate without sentimentality, restless, difficult to offend, and unwilling to preserve anything merely because it is beloved.
- **Symbol:** three receding wave-lines crossing a single footprint.
- **Philosophy:** survival is not remaining unchanged; grief is the shape love takes while learning to move.
- **Divine Realm:** **The Thousand Estuaries**, where rivers from impossible histories meet a sea that returns travelers altered but recognizable.
- **Relationships:** close to Maereth's cycles and Ilyra's travelers; mistrusts Kharum's attachment to permanence. Thessa understood Eidren's desire for change but opposed possibilities that prevented any stable return.
- **Divine War:** maintained evacuation routes between failing realms, sometimes transporting enemies together. Thessa supported the Veiling only after the Unwritten Country began returning refugees as several incompatible selves.
- **Modern worship:** sailors, ferrymen, migrants, mourners, midwives, scouts, and communities rebuilding after disaster. Worship emphasizes processions, shared meals after funerals, and objects deliberately surrendered to water.
- **Civilizational influence:** navigation, water law, migration compacts, adoption customs, flood engineering, mourning rites, and the acceptance that identity can change without becoming false.
- **Patron races:** the Karakai, amphibious and crustacean peoples whose cities migrate along tidal shelves and remember history through route patterns rather than permanent capitals.
- **Visual identity:** layered shell, blue-green ceramic, silvered rope, movable walls, tide marks incorporated into decoration, and structures designed to flood safely.

**Why it strengthens the game:** Thessa ties travel, preparation, loss, and cultural change together. Her ruins can move or become accessible only under particular conditions.

**Natural discovery:** drowned road markers with newer names above older ones; Karakai buildings designed to be dismantled; mourning objects returning upstream years later.

## 2.3 Orun, Keeper of the Last Name

- **Domains:** death, ancestry, inheritance, burial, endings, memory entrusted to others.
- **Personality:** courteous, grave, unexpectedly tender, intolerant of waste, and hostile to anyone who confuses preservation with ownership.
- **Symbol:** a closed eye above a name-line ending in a seed.
- **Philosophy:** an ending gives shape to what preceded it. The dead must be remembered accurately enough to be released, not kept useful forever.
- **Divine Realm:** **The Quiet Continuance**, a landscape of houses, archives, gardens, and roads constructed from what the dead willingly leave behind.
- **Relationships:** works closely with Maereth and Kharum; distrusts Nymbr's edited dreams and Avarr's public ownership of memory. Orun defended Eidren's right of souls to refuse divine claim.
- **Divine War:** remained neutral until both sides began restoring dead soldiers without their consent. Orun then destroyed resurrection engines belonging to every faction. Orun accepted the Veiling only if mortal souls retained final refusal.
- **Modern worship:** undertakers, genealogists, Gravecallers, physicians, heirs, archivists, and communities guarding names erased by conquerors.
- **Civilizational influence:** inheritance law, cemeteries, ancestor testimony, medical ethics, wills, memorial craft, and prohibitions against compelled resurrection.
- **Patron races:** the Namar, a nearly vanished people able to entrust portions of memory to descendants. Their mixed descendants appear in old mortuary families, including disputed Veyr lines.
- **Visual identity:** dark green-black stone, pale wood, silver name strips, low doorways, enclosed gardens, and empty chairs built into tombs.

**Why it strengthens the game:** Orun makes death magic morally complex. Gravecalling can preserve testimony or violate release; neither visual darkness nor undead form determines morality.

**Natural discovery:** tombs that open only when an heir relinquishes an item; spirits asking for a false heroic epitaph to be corrected; weapons growing weaker when stripped of former owners' names.

## 2.4 Maereth, the Many-Rooted

- **Domains:** growth, hunger, reciprocity, decay, kinship, ecological succession.
- **Personality:** generous, unsentimental, communal, fiercely protective of diversity, and capable of terrifying correction when one life consumes without return.
- **Symbol:** branching roots surrounding a bite-shaped absence.
- **Philosophy:** everything lives by taking; morality begins with what is returned and whether anything else is permitted to grow.
- **Divine Realm:** **The Living Exchange**, an ecosystem of forests, fungal continents, mobile gardens, predators, and cities that compost and rebuild themselves.
- **Relationships:** closest to Thessa and Orun; argues with Kharum over cities that deny ecological debt. Maereth admired Eidren's defense of variation but opposed selfhood severed from reciprocity.
- **Divine War:** protected mortal seed-vaults and species refuges. Some of Maereth's Primordials became monstrous after deciding civilization itself was parasitic. Maereth helped bind them rather than denying responsibility.
- **Modern worship:** farmers, healers, hunters, cooks, herbalists, Thornbinders, midwives, and communities managing common land.
- **Civilizational influence:** crop rotation, commons law, medicine, food taboos, mutual-aid houses, controlled burns, funerary planting, and limits on extraction.
- **Patron races:** Myconids were shaped directly as distributed persons; several smaller plantlike peoples descend from Maereth's lesser divine offspring. Elves later formed important covenants with these peoples but belong to Nymbr's lineage.
- **Visual identity:** living timber, cultivated moss, bone fertilizer inlays, asymmetric courtyards, red-green sap glass, visible repair grafts, and no clean boundary between garden and building.

**Why it strengthens the game:** Maereth makes nature neither pure nor gentle. Healing, poison, hunger, and decay belong to one moral economy, reinforcing crafting and survival.

**Natural discovery:** orchards thriving around honest graves but failing around hidden mass burials; medicinal roots that become poisonous when harvested without reseeding; living doors remembering generations of touch.

## 2.5 Kharum, the Weight-Bearer

- **Domains:** measure, craft, pressure, endurance, architecture, material truth.
- **Personality:** slow to trust, dryly humorous, proud of competent work, and merciless toward grand designs that conceal structural cost.
- **Symbol:** a suspended weight above an unfinished arch.
- **Philosophy:** what cannot bear its consequence was never complete. Beauty is the visible resolution of pressure.
- **Divine Realm:** **The Bearing Deep**, a world of mines, furnaces, bridges, workshops, and immense structures under purposeful strain.
- **Relationships:** respects Avarr's accountability and Orun's inheritance; disputes Thessa's impermanence and Nymbr's preference for ambiguous form. Kharum opposed Eidren when tools and fortifications began refusing essential functions unpredictably.
- **Divine War:** built the anchors that prevented the Physical Realm from splitting into competing histories. Kharum also forged several prisons later abused by the victors, including structures involved in Malakar's confinement.
- **Modern worship:** builders, miners, smiths, engineers, masons, armorers, accountants, and labor guilds.
- **Civilizational influence:** standards of weight, road grades, durable archives, worker oaths, structural inspection, coinage, siegecraft, and the belief that maintenance is a sacred civic duty.
- **Patron races:** dwarves, created to perceive pressure, fault, and material fatigue as intuitively as humans read expression.
- **Visual identity:** basalt, ironwood, exposed joints, counterweights, deep amber furnace light, load-bearing sculpture, and repairs dated rather than hidden.

**Why it strengthens the game:** Kharum turns crafting and architecture into lore. Players can read who built a ruin, how it failed, and what load still waits behind a cracked wall.

**Natural discovery:** bridges whose repair marks form dynastic histories; legendary armor with better-maintained mundane straps than ornament; collapsed temples that prove their doctrine ignored material cost.

## 2.6 Varka, Keeper of the Red Accord

- **Domains:** conflict, courage, boundaries, defense, consequence, honorable surrender.
- **Personality:** direct, disciplined, impatient with moral performance, protective of worthy enemies, and susceptible to believing decisive violence can simplify political failure.
- **Symbol:** two opposed blades meeting above an open gate.
- **Philosophy:** conflict reveals boundaries, but victory creates obligations toward the defeated. Violence without an achievable peace is appetite disguised as courage.
- **Divine Realm:** **The Contested Plain**, a realm of shifting borders, fortified refuges, training grounds, and halls where former enemies negotiate the consequences of battle.
- **Relationships:** frequently clashes with Avarr over process and with Ilyra over compromise; respects Kharum's fortifications and Thessa's evacuation work. Varka admired Eidren's refusal to submit but condemned refusal that made surrender impossible.
- **Divine War:** commanded the largest loyalist armies and created siege-breaking Primordials. Varka's forces saved mortal cities and committed catastrophic reprisals. Varka later demanded records of both, creating tension with the Veiling.
- **Modern worship:** soldiers, guards, rebels, duelists, negotiators, refugees defending settlements, and Orc oath-clans.
- **Civilizational influence:** rules of surrender, protected evacuation corridors, militia training, limits on siege, blood-price systems, challenge rituals, and civic defense obligations.
- **Patron races:** Orcs, designed for rapid communal coordination under threat and a powerful sensitivity to broken terms of surrender.
- **Visual identity:** red oxide steel, dark leather, paired gates, removable banners, scarred surfaces left visible, circular mustering yards, and weapons displayed beside peace terms.

**Why it strengthens the game:** Varka prevents warfare from becoming an evil aesthetic. The Order, armies, rebels, and defenders can be judged by objectives and aftermath rather than faction color.

**Natural discovery:** forts with protected enemy cemeteries; surrendered weapons converted into gate hinges; massacre sites where Varkan symbols were deliberately chiseled away by Varka's own priests.

## 2.7 Ilyra, Lady of the Open Table

- **Domains:** exchange, language, hospitality, roads, negotiation, mutual dependence.
- **Personality:** curious, sociable, strategically generous, difficult to deceive twice, and too willing to treat every value as negotiable.
- **Symbol:** an open table crossed by a road with seats on both sides.
- **Philosophy:** strangers become less dangerous when each has something the other must willingly provide. Hospitality is a temporary peace, not automatic friendship.
- **Divine Realm:** **The Crossroads Without Center**, an endless network of markets, kitchens, embassies, hostels, and roads whose destinations depend on what travelers carry and offer.
- **Relationships:** close to Thessa; useful to every God; distrusted by Varka for bargaining during emergencies and by Orun for trafficking in memory. Ilyra defended Eidren's right to exit contracts but insisted departure must acknowledge incurred cost.
- **Divine War:** kept negotiations alive and smuggled civilians, relics, and messages across all fronts. Ilyran networks also profited from scarcity and moved weapons that prolonged the conflict.
- **Modern worship:** merchants, translators, innkeepers, diplomats, caravaners, cooks, messengers, smugglers, and border communities.
- **Civilizational influence:** trade languages, guest-right, market weights shared across cultures, neutral roads, ransom customs, caravan law, and diplomatic immunity.
- **Patron races:** the Serekin, a diasporic people whose identity is maintained through traveling household-tables and reciprocal host lineages rather than territory.
- **Visual identity:** modular arcades, painted route tiles, brass tokens, shared courtyards, many doors, fabric canopies, and inscriptions in parallel languages.

**Why it strengthens the game:** Ilyra sacralizes travel, supplies, and negotiation without pretending commerce is benevolent. Roads and markets become political institutions.

**Natural discovery:** abandoned inns where guest-right still restrains hostile spirits; route tokens accepted by enemies who share no language; trade shrines recording prices paid during famines.

## 2.8 Nymbr, the Veiled Eye

- **Domains:** dreams, secrecy, imagination, warning, pattern, merciful uncertainty.
- **Personality:** reserved, playful in unsettling ways, compassionate toward private weakness, and capable of justifying manipulation as protection from unbearable knowledge.
- **Symbol:** an eye reflected in dark water, with the reflection looking elsewhere.
- **Philosophy:** not every truth should arrive before a mind can survive it. Concealment is moral only while it protects another's future ability to choose.
- **Divine Realm:** **The House Behind Sleep**, a shifting realm of observatories, theaters, memory chambers, symbolic landscapes, and doors opened by questions rather than keys.
- **Relationships:** perpetual tension with Avarr; affinity with Orun's private memories and Maereth's dreaming ecosystems. Nymbr understood Eidren best and supplied methods later used to erase Eidren, an act Nymbr considers both necessary and unforgivable.
- **Divine War:** coordinated warnings through dreams and concealed refugee routes. Nymbr also designed the mnemonic architecture of the Veiling and ensured even the Eight could not remember every detail at once.
- **Modern worship:** seers, artists, spies, therapists, navigators, storytellers, secret-keepers, and those who guard dangerous knowledge.
- **Civilizational influence:** privacy customs, sealed archives, dream diagnosis, theater, coded maps, intelligence networks, initiation rites, and graded access to hazardous truths.
- **Patron races:** elves, shaped for long memory, symbolic perception, and deliberate forgetting. Their cultures disagree bitterly over whether forgetting can ever be sacred.
- **Visual identity:** dark blue stone, mirrored water, masked windows, star apertures, layered curtains, misleading symmetry, and images whose meaning changes with viewing angle.

**Why it strengthens the game:** Nymbr makes mystery an ethical practice rather than authorial withholding. Hidden truths can be protected, exploited, or revealed too early.

**Natural discovery:** murals visible only in reflection; archives whose censored spaces form a second text; dreams that warn accurately through symbols but never dictate interpretation.

## 2.9 The Eight as a civilizational system

No healthy civilization can embody only one God.

- Avarr without Nymbr becomes surveillance.
- Nymbr without Avarr becomes unaccountable manipulation.
- Kharum without Thessa becomes brittle permanence.
- Thessa without Kharum becomes rootless instability.
- Varka without Ilyra makes every disagreement a border war.
- Ilyra without Varka sells boundaries that should be defended.
- Maereth without Orun refuses necessary endings.
- Orun without Maereth preserves a world that no longer grows.
- All Eight without Eidren can make a perfectly ordered prison.
- Eidren without the Eight makes continuity optional.

This interdependence is the theological foundation for moral ambiguity. Every faction can possess a real virtue and become dangerous by elevating it beyond correction.

# PART 3 – Primordials

## 3.1 Definition

Primordials are self-aware divine beings formed within a Divine Realm or from concentrated divine Essence. They possess identity, memory, preference, and agency. They are not mortal souls promoted after death, although a mortal and Primordial can become entangled.

Every Primordial has:

- a **lineage**, identifying the God and realm-principle from which it arose;
- a **purpose**, the relationship it was made to maintain;
- a **mantle**, the authority and capabilities it currently carries;
- a **name**, recording identity and obligations;
- an **anchor pattern**, determining how it can manifest in Runalis.

Purpose influences a Primordial but does not eliminate choice. Eidren's legacy means any self-aware Primordial can deviate. Deviation may be moral growth, corruption, necessary adaptation, or catastrophic abandonment.

## 3.2 Hierarchy

The hierarchy describes origin and authority, not moral worth.

### Primearchs

The greatest Primordials, directly invested with a portion of a God's sovereign authority. They can speak for a Realm, create lesser Primordials with permission, alter Concordant Ways, and change how a divine principle is administered. Each God maintains one to five.

### Mantled Primordials

Direct creations of a God or beings personally granted a lasting office. They administer major processes such as a migration current, ancestral archive, fortress-boundary, or species covenant. Malakar is of this rank by origin, though accumulated power makes him stronger than many Primearchs in direct combat.

### Shaped Primordials

Created by Primearchs, Mantled Primordials, or realm institutions to perform bounded work. Many serve as guides, wardens, envoys, artisans, predators, or custodians. They can grow beyond their design but do not inherit sovereign authority automatically.

### Echo-Born

Persons coalesced from repeated mortal practice where a Divine Realm presses against Runalis. A road used under guest-right for centuries might produce an Ilyran guide. A battlefield where surrender was repeatedly honored might produce a Varkan guardian. They are fully personal despite lacking a single creator.

### Severed Primordials

Any Primordial cut off from its Realm, name, or office. “Severed” describes condition, not allegiance. Some become mortal protectors; others consume Essence desperately, deform landscapes, or attach themselves to cults to maintain identity.

## 3.3 Creation

Primordials arise through four canonical methods:

1. **Direct shaping:** a God separates divine Essence, gives it name and purpose, and accepts a corresponding limitation or cost.
2. **Delegated shaping:** a Primearch creates a bounded being using authority granted by its God.
3. **Realm emergence:** a recurring process within a Divine Realm becomes self-aware.
4. **Faith condensation:** sustained mortal practice aligns a threshold until an Echo-Born person forms.

Faith can influence form, vocabulary, and access. It cannot produce sovereign divine Essence from nothing. A village may cause an existing protective current to become “the Lantern Widow,” but belief alone cannot make a new God.

## 3.4 Death and return

A Primordial has three kinds of death:

- **Embodied death:** its physical vessel is destroyed. Most Essence retreats toward its Realm if a route remains.
- **Name-death:** its identity and obligations are broken apart. Essence survives but may return as fragments, instincts, relics, or several successor persons.
- **Root-death:** its defining pattern is destroyed within its Divine Realm. This is the closest to permanent death and can damage the process it maintained.

Primordials do not reincarnate automatically. Return requires sufficient surviving name, Essence, purpose, and a route. A returned Primordial is continuous with its former self only to the extent those elements remain. Worshippers may unknowingly reconstruct a different person around familiar fragments.

This prevents death from becoming meaningless while supporting recurring myths, corrupted returns, relic bosses, and disputed identity.

## 3.5 Manifestation in Runalis

Primordials enter the Physical Realm through:

- temporary projection;
- a prepared vessel;
- possession with consent or coercion;
- a bloodline descendant;
- a relic containing part of their name;
- an aligned location or repeated rite;
- a breach in a Concordant Way;
- forced summoning.

Every method imposes constraints. A projected Primordial may speak but cannot lift a weapon. A vessel grants mobility but limits power. A relic permits one repeated act. A fully opened threshold produces great power but begins transforming the region into the corresponding Divine Realm.

## 3.6 Why they rarely remain

Divine Distance forces a choice:

- retreat before the anchor fails;
- accept increasing mortality and local limitation;
- consume Essence to sustain divinity;
- transform the environment into a realm-extension.

This is why old Primordial occupations leave distinctive landscapes and why permanent divine “NPCs” are rare. A being that has remained for centuries has paid a profound cost or imposed one on its surroundings.

## 3.7 Mortals, worship, and politics

Primordials may love mortals, resent them, study them, depend on them, or regard them as dangerous sources of reinterpretation. Worship gives a Primordial stable routes and a social identity. It also pressures the being to perform the role worshippers expect.

A feared Primordial can become more monstrous because every invocation prepares only monstrous forms. A locally beloved one can be trapped in an obsolete role. Primordials therefore participate in temple politics, cultivate competing traditions, destroy inaccurate icons, or encourage ambiguity to preserve freedom.

Some cultures worship Primordials as gods. This is not simply ignorance: a local Primordial may be the only divine power that has answered for a thousand years.

## 3.8 Bloodlines

Nephilim, Cambion, and Vampire bloodlines descend from Divine War entanglements, not from generic angelic, demonic, or undead categories.

- **Nephilim:** descendants or vessels carrying stable Avarran or other high-realm Primordial Essence. Their gifts intensify witnessed purpose and can erode private identity if treated as proof of holiness.
- **Cambion:** descendants of war-line Primordials, most often Varkan Severed lineages, whose embodied Essence adapted through hunger, heat, and conflict. Their power is not evidence of evil or worship.
- **Vampire:** mortals altered by damaged Orunic continuity rites that preserved appetite and memory while interrupting natural release. Some lines were deliberate survival covenants; others were punishments or failed medicine.

Bloodlines are history carried in bodies. Choosing or awakening one should reveal obligations, enemies, lost techniques, and contested ancestry as well as combat power.

## 3.9 Malakar's rank

Malakar was directly shaped by Varka as a Mantled Primordial called **the Breaker of Final Sieges**. His purpose was to destroy fortifications whose defenders had rejected every surrender while civilians remained trapped inside.

He accumulated exceptional power by consuming broken boundary-oaths and fragments of divine prisons during the War. He knows how Kharum's bindings, Avarr's oaths, and Nymbr's memory seals interact. This knowledge makes him invaluable to the Order of the Last Dawn.

Malakar is not a Primearch because Varka never invested him with sovereign authority. He cannot speak for the Contested Plain, appoint divine offices, alter Varka's law, or create a legitimate lineage of Primordials. He is a weapon who became a person and then a prisoner—not a viceroy of a God.

The distinction matters narratively:

- defeating Malakar proves the player can survive concentrated divine force;
- opposing Seraphel means confronting delegated cosmic authority backed by a functioning Realm;
- Malakar can reveal truths but cannot revoke Seraphel's office or fully understand Avarr's current intent;
- cultures may fear Malakar more immediately while structuring entire governments around Seraphel's authority.

## 3.10 Natural player discovery

- different summon sites embody different anchor methods;
- a recurring “same” Primordial has changed vocabulary and preferences, raising identity questions;
- bloodline relics respond to lineage rather than moral behavior;
- long-embodied beings show hunger, scars, habits, and local attachments;
- temples argue over images because each image stabilizes a different manifestation;
- Malakar's prison contains no Primearch throne, seal of delegation, or realm-key—only weapon restraints and consumed oaths.

# PART 4 – Primearchs

## 4.1 The Primearch office

A Primearch receives a **Crown of Office**, an indivisible portion of a God's administrative authority. “Crown” is metaphysical and need not be a physical crown. It allows the Primearch to interpret divine law within a mandate.

A God may maintain between one and five Primearchs because each Crown limits the authority the God retains directly. Creating many improves reach but fragments policy. Removing a Crown without consent can wound the Realm.

Primearchs can be defeated in battle, but power alone does not transfer office. A usurper must obtain recognition from the God, the Realm, or a sufficient set of foundational treaties. This is why Malakar cannot become a Primearch by killing one.

## 4.2 Primearchs of Avarr

### Seraphel, the Hand Beneath the Falling

- **Purpose:** mercy exercised through protection; authority to intervene when lawful institutions fail those they govern.
- **Personality:** compassionate, patient, attentive, and terrifyingly willing to remove freedom when he concludes freedom has become an engine of preventable suffering.
- **History:** Seraphel saved cities on every side of the Divine War, accepted refugees regardless of allegiance, and repeatedly defied Avarran courts that prioritized legitimate process over immediate life. His interventions made him the most beloved surviving Primearch.
- **God relationship:** Avarr loves and distrusts him. Seraphel believes Avarr mistakes accountability for moral adequacy; Avarr fears mercy that cannot be refused.
- **Other Primearchs:** close to Thessa's Neralai; bitterly opposed to Nymbr's Veyu, who helped conceal the costs of some rescues; Malakar knows Seraphel once approved a binding experiment erased from public record.
- **Current status:** active, embodied through carefully managed manifestations, and publicly aligned with the Covenant.
- **Public reputation:** humanity's divine protector, healer of wars, and proof that power can kneel to the suffering.
- **Mythology:** said to catch falling cities in his wings. Older images show no wings, only many supporting hands; later iconography simplified these into radiant pinions.
- **Continuing influence:** enormous. Covenant doctrine, military intervention, hospitals, and Zephyr's public legitimacy all borrow from Seraphel's image.

Seraphel is one of the greatest surviving Primearchs. He is not secretly cruel. His antagonism grows from a defensible conclusion: if suffering repeatedly arises from choice, mercy may require managing choice. This makes Malakar's warning difficult to accept.

### Caldris, Keeper of the Unburned Record

- **Purpose:** preserve testimony through catastrophe without deciding what later generations must conclude.
- **Personality:** restrained, skeptical, meticulous, and quietly furious at censorship.
- **History:** carried contradictory archives out of reality fractures and discovered that some mutually exclusive records were all genuine. Caldris opposed the complete Veiling but helped design sealed records that could survive it.
- **God relationship:** loyal to Avarr but refuses direct commands to destroy testimony.
- **Other Primearchs:** cooperates with Orun's Edrane and distrusts Veyu. Seraphel regards Caldris as compassionate toward records and cold toward living urgency.
- **Current status:** missing. Portions of the Unburned Record appear in scattered ruin-vaults.
- **Public reputation:** an obscure patron of archivists, often reduced to a saint.
- **Mythology:** a librarian who walked through fire while every lie burned from the shelves. The myth is false; Caldris preserved lies because lies are evidence.
- **Continuing influence:** hidden archives, testimony relics, and anti-Covenant scholars use Caldris's methods.

### Othra Vale, the Empty Seat

- **Purpose:** ensure authority can be surrendered, transferred, or left vacant rather than becoming permanent possession.
- **Personality:** formal, self-effacing, stubborn, and suspicious of charismatic leaders.
- **History:** ended three divine occupations by forcing victorious governors to relinquish emergency power. During the Veiling, Othra insisted that no God inherit Eidren's authority.
- **God relationship:** trusted by Avarr with succession law; rarely agrees with Seraphel.
- **Other Primearchs:** respected by Ilyra's Samat and Varka's Korveth.
- **Current status:** dormant within an unoccupied throne-route; awakens when a legitimate office cannot be peacefully vacated.
- **Public reputation:** nearly forgotten outside abdication rites.
- **Mythology:** rulers leave one empty chair at coronations, believing death sits there. It actually represents the possibility that nobody has the right to rule.
- **Continuing influence:** constitutional customs, regencies, and hidden resistance to permanent Covenant emergency powers.

## 4.3 Primearchs of Thessa

### Neralai, Who Returns Changed

- **Purpose:** protect continuity through migration, transformation, and grief.
- **Personality:** warm, pragmatic, curious, and incapable of promising restoration without change.
- **History:** maintained the largest refugee Way during the Divine War and fused incompatible communities into new nations rather than returning them to destroyed homelands.
- **God relationship:** Thessa's closest confidant and frequent field representative.
- **Other Primearchs:** allied with Seraphel during evacuations; opposed Kharum's Dhom Var when he tried to hold an unsalvageable city.
- **Current status:** active in the Thousand Estuaries; manifests briefly at mass migrations.
- **Public reputation:** ferryman, mother of exiles, or omen of irreversible change depending on culture.
- **Mythology:** anyone who crosses Neralai's wake loses one memory and gains one future. This describes grief rituals, not a literal bargain.
- **Continuing influence:** migration compacts, Karakai route-cities, refugee customs, and flood-revealed ruins.

### Urosh of the Low Water

- **Purpose:** reveal what prosperity, flood, or emotion has concealed when conditions recede.
- **Personality:** austere, patient, and more interested in exposed foundations than punishment.
- **History:** lowered divine seas to reveal supply crimes and drowned prisons. Caused famine once by exposing too much waterbed at once and never denied responsibility.
- **God relationship:** Thessa values Urosh's honesty but limits his authority.
- **Other Primearchs:** works with Caldris and Edrane; disliked by Ilyran powers whose trade secrets wash ashore.
- **Current status:** fragmented but alive, appearing in droughts and receding marshes.
- **Public reputation:** blamed for drought, invoked by investigators and grave-searchers.
- **Mythology:** the Low Water exposes one truth for every well that fails.
- **Continuing influence:** submerged dungeons, seasonal routes, revealed mass graves, and resources accessible only at environmental thresholds.

## 4.4 Primearchs of Orun

### Edrane, the Pallkeeper

- **Purpose:** guard the boundary between remembrance and possession of the dead.
- **Personality:** gentle, uncompromising, and patient with grief but merciless toward coercive resurrection.
- **History:** destroyed both loyalist and Eidren-aligned death engines. Negotiated the clause that dispersed refusal into mortal souls during the Veiling.
- **God relationship:** Orun's most trusted executor.
- **Other Primearchs:** aligned with Othra Vale and opposed Maereth's Sovael when Sovael tried to recycle unwilling dead into a living city.
- **Current status:** active but rarely manifest; acts through funerary custodians and Gravecaller lineages.
- **Public reputation:** death judge in some cultures, compassionate undertaker in others.
- **Mythology:** Edrane weighs whether a soul deserves rest. Canonically, Edrane protects the soul's ability to refuse continued use.
- **Continuing influence:** anti-necromancy laws, ethical Gravecalling, sealed mortuary dungeons, and Vampire disputes.

### Thren Veyr, Bearer of Inheritance

- **Purpose:** ensure power, memory, and debt pass honestly between generations.
- **Personality:** proud, melancholic, politically astute, and overly tolerant of hereditary institutions that preserve obligations well.
- **History:** helped establish early mortal dynasties as containers for dangerous relic duties. The Veyr name descends from a mortal house entrusted with one fragment of his office, not from direct divine parentage.
- **God relationship:** Orun considers him useful and dangerously enamored of continuity.
- **Other Primearchs:** frequently debated Othra Vale over inherited office.
- **Current status:** name-dead. His mantle persists in divided Veyr relics and bloodlines.
- **Public reputation:** remembered as a founder-king, ancestor, vampire progenitor, or curse.
- **Mythology:** House Veyr claims his blood. The more accurate truth is an inherited obligation that altered blood over centuries.
- **Continuing influence:** Castle Veyr, mortuary nobility, disputed succession relics, and Vampire bloodline lore.

## 4.5 Primearchs of Maereth

### Sovael, Garden of Many Bodies

- **Purpose:** preserve ecological diversity and reciprocal kinship across species.
- **Personality:** nurturing, alien in scale, humorous through many voices, and hostile to exclusive ownership of land.
- **History:** maintained refuge ecosystems during the War. Later attempted to incorporate willing mortals into a shared organism; disagreement over whether consent could be withdrawn caused a violent schism.
- **God relationship:** beloved but closely watched by Maereth.
- **Other Primearchs:** respects Neralai; was opposed by Edrane over treatment of the dead.
- **Current status:** sleeping beneath several disconnected forests that are secretly one organism.
- **Public reputation:** world-tree, forest mother, consuming hive, or lost elven homeland.
- **Mythology:** every ancient forest is one of Sovael's dreams. Some truly are connected.
- **Continuing influence:** Myconid politics, elven memory groves, living dungeons, and landscapes responding to collective treatment.

### Rauk, the Necessary Hunger

- **Purpose:** prevent living systems from avoiding consumption, predation, and limits.
- **Personality:** candid, disciplined, contemptuous of waste, and surprisingly protective of prey populations.
- **History:** culled divine organisms that threatened to consume realms. During the War, followers used his doctrine to justify atrocities; Rauk hunted those followers personally.
- **God relationship:** Maereth needs and dislikes him.
- **Other Primearchs:** respects Varka's Korveth and detests Malakar's consumption of broken oaths because it returns nothing.
- **Current status:** active but unworshipped in most settled regions.
- **Public reputation:** monster, famine spirit, patron of ethical hunters.
- **Mythology:** Rauk eats the last member of every species. Canonically, he protects against any species becoming the only one left.
- **Continuing influence:** apex bosses, hunting rites, resource depletion, and ancient culling sites.

## 4.6 Primearchs of Kharum

### Dhom Var, the Unbroken Span

- **Purpose:** maintain structures and agreements that must bear civilization across catastrophe.
- **Personality:** reliable, proud, conservative, and slow to accept that endurance can become imprisonment.
- **History:** anchored the Physical Realm during the Divine War and built evacuation bridges between histories. He also held one city beyond viable rescue, causing those inside to survive as immobile stone-consciousness.
- **God relationship:** Kharum's most powerful builder and greatest cautionary example.
- **Other Primearchs:** old conflict with Neralai; collaborated with Avarr's Caldris.
- **Current status:** embodied as part of a shattered trans-realm bridge.
- **Public reputation:** divine engineer, mountain giant, patron of impossible bridges.
- **Mythology:** no bridge bearing his mark can fall. They can fail by becoming something other than a bridge.
- **Continuing influence:** ancient infrastructure, deep roads, structural puzzles, and dormant bridge-minds.

### Avessa, She Who Tests the Join

- **Purpose:** reveal hidden weakness before consequence does.
- **Personality:** skeptical, precise, blunt, and delighted by repairs better than originals.
- **History:** inspected divine weapons and exposed flaws leaders preferred hidden. Her refusal to certify Kharum's prison design delayed Malakar's confinement; the Eight used a compromised version anyway.
- **God relationship:** trusted critic whose dissent Kharum treats as sacred.
- **Other Primearchs:** respected by Othra Vale; disliked by Seraphel when urgent mercy bypasses testing.
- **Current status:** active through rare avatars among craftspeople.
- **Public reputation:** patron of apprentices, saboteur, or spirit of bad luck before completion.
- **Mythology:** Avessa breaks one tool from every masterwork set. In truth, craftsmen break test pieces in her honor.
- **Continuing influence:** crafting trials, fault-reading, legendary upgrades, and evidence within Malakar's prison.

## 4.7 Primearchs of Varka

### Korveth, Shield After Victory

- **Purpose:** impose obligations on victors and protect the defeated during transition.
- **Personality:** severe, honorable, weary, and more suspicious of triumphant allies than enemies.
- **History:** ended massacres committed by Varka's own forces and executed commanders who ignored surrender. Helped Othra Vale dismantle emergency regimes.
- **God relationship:** Varka's conscience and frequent accuser.
- **Other Primearchs:** allied with Othra and Edrane; considers Seraphel's protection dangerously paternal.
- **Current status:** missing after entering a mortal war whose victors erased all records of surrender.
- **Public reputation:** shield-saint, traitor to victory, or judge of generals.
- **Mythology:** Korveth appears behind every defeated soldier spared by an enemy.
- **Continuing influence:** military law, haunted forts, surrender relics, and Order of the Last Dawn doctrine.

### Iskara, the Last Challenge

- **Purpose:** ensure boundaries and claims can be tested without total war.
- **Personality:** proud, theatrical, perceptive, and genuinely committed to accepting loss.
- **History:** resolved divine territorial conflicts through bounded contests. Lost a challenge to an Eidren-aligned Primordial and honored the result even when it cost Varka a strategic gate.
- **God relationship:** beloved by Varka, criticized for making spectacle of serious disputes.
- **Other Primearchs:** friendly rivalry with Ilyra's Samat.
- **Current status:** active in the Contested Plain; rarely answers modern duelists who seek personal glory.
- **Public reputation:** patron of champions and trial by combat.
- **Mythology:** defeating Iskara grants any wish. Canonically, victory grants recognition of one bounded claim.
- **Continuing influence:** boss challenge traditions, arena law, champion relics, and nonlethal conflict structures.

## 4.8 Primearchs of Ilyra

### Samat of the Seven Tables

- **Purpose:** establish exchange between peoples who share no trust, language, or value system.
- **Personality:** convivial, calculating, endlessly patient, and offended by concealed external costs.
- **History:** negotiated seven foundational interrealm treaties. During the War, one treaty market became a weapons exchange that prolonged three campaigns; Samat closed it and accepted blame.
- **God relationship:** Ilyra's chief negotiator and occasional restraint.
- **Other Primearchs:** respects Iskara and Othra; bargains uneasily with Nymbr's Veyu.
- **Current status:** active through seven mobile threshold-tables, several lost in Runalis.
- **Public reputation:** merchant god, first innkeeper, or demon of contracts in cultures harmed by trade.
- **Mythology:** any enemies who eat at all seven tables become kin. The tables instead create seven escalating obligations.
- **Continuing influence:** caravan law, multilingual relics, hidden markets, and diplomatic dungeons.

### Pel Aro, Keeper of the Unowned Road

- **Purpose:** maintain routes no single state, temple, or army may permanently control.
- **Personality:** solitary, practical, suspicious of maps, and fiercely hospitable at thresholds.
- **History:** broke sieges by opening neutral roads and destroyed toll empires that violated passage compacts. Sometimes opened routes that also enabled invasion.
- **God relationship:** Ilyra values the roads and questions the refusal of governance.
- **Other Primearchs:** close to Neralai; repeatedly fought Dhom Var over permanent gates.
- **Current status:** Severed by choice, walking Runalis in a diminished mortal form according to several traditions.
- **Public reputation:** road ghost, wandering guide, patron of smugglers and refugees.
- **Mythology:** a ninth road appears to travelers with no acceptable destination.
- **Continuing influence:** secret routes, changing maps, guest-right camps, and paths around Covenant control.

## 4.9 Primearchs of Nymbr

### Veyu, Architect of Kind Concealments

- **Purpose:** delay dangerous truth until recipients retain the capacity to respond freely.
- **Personality:** empathetic, manipulative, burdened, and certain that timing can matter more than disclosure.
- **History:** concealed refugee routes and traumatic memories during the War, then designed the mnemonic machinery of the Veiling. Veyu hid parts of the method from Nymbr and from themself.
- **God relationship:** Nymbr's closest collaborator and living guilt.
- **Other Primearchs:** opposed by Caldris; Seraphel used Veyu's methods to soften memories after catastrophic rescues.
- **Current status:** divided into several masked persons, none knowing whether it is the original.
- **Public reputation:** dream healer, liar-saint, masked librarian.
- **Mythology:** Veyu takes painful memories and returns them when they become useful. Sometimes the returned memory belongs to someone else.
- **Continuing influence:** hidden-order memory techniques, sealed archives, dream dungeons, and unreliable witnesses.

### Oryn, the Sleepless Map

- **Purpose:** perceive patterns of danger before they become inevitable.
- **Personality:** anxious, brilliant, compassionate at distance, and prone to valuing predicted lives over present individuals.
- **History:** forecast numerous Divine War catastrophes, but attempts to prevent them sometimes caused alternate disasters. Oryn mapped the first signs that Eidren's removal would leave an Interstice.
- **God relationship:** Nymbr consults but does not command Oryn.
- **Other Primearchs:** works with Avessa; distrusted by Varka's Primearchs for treating war as geometry.
- **Current status:** trapped in a self-updating map distributed across observatories and living dreams.
- **Public reputation:** constellation, oracle, cursed atlas.
- **Mythology:** Oryn's complete map shows the reader's death. It more dangerously shows deaths the reader might cause by avoiding it.
- **Continuing influence:** changing maps, Watcher traditions, predictive ruins, and exploration routes that respond to player history.

## 4.10 Primearch discovery principles

Primearchs should be understood through civilizational consequences before boss encounters or direct appearances.

- Seraphel is first known through hospitals, protected roads, veteran testimony, and cities he genuinely saved.
- Dhom Var is recognized in impossible infrastructure before his name appears.
- Veyu is inferred from recurring styles of compassionate censorship.
- Edrane is present in burial customs that preserve refusal.
- Korveth survives in enemy cemeteries maintained by victors.

A Primearch appearance should change laws, borders, worship, or the function of a region. It should never feel like merely a larger monster.

# PART 5 – Historical Timeline

## 5.1 Dating conventions

Modern Covenant calendars count **After Concordance (AC)**, claiming year 0 marks the final agreement of the Eight after a war against unnamed “lawless powers.” Restricted scholars call the same event the Veiling.

Dates before 0 AC are approximate because Divine War fractures produced incompatible chronologies. “BV” means **Before Veiling** and is used only in the development bible and forbidden archives.

The present campaign begins in **1847 AC**.

## 5.2 The pre-mortal ages

### Unmeasured duration — The Unbounded

No stable time existed. Archaeological evidence is impossible, but relics exposed to the Interstice sometimes contain materials with contradictory ages.

**Development opportunities:** impossible resources, artifacts older than duration, dungeons that change causal order.

### Approximately 12,000–10,000 BV — The First Keeping

The Nine establish the Physical Realm, their Divine Realms, and the first Concordant Ways. Runalis becomes stable enough for persistent matter and embodied life.

**World traces:** ninefold foundations beneath later eightfold temples; realm-junction machines; materials that embody more than one divine principle.

### Approximately 10,000–8,700 BV — The Shaping of Primordials

The Gods create the earliest Mantled Primordials. Primearch offices emerge after the Gods discover that direct intervention destabilizes the Physical Realm. The first Crowns of Office are negotiated rather than imposed.

**World traces:** pre-iconic Primordial sites without humanoid imagery; Crown seals; workshops where divine purposes were bounded.

### Approximately 8,700–7,200 BV — The First Peoples

The Gods and lesser divine offspring shape intelligent mortal peoples. Early races are not isolated experiments; they meet through Ilyran roads and Thessan migrations almost immediately.

Humans, Karakai, Namar, Myconids, dwarves, Orcs, Serekin, and elves establish distinct relationships with mortality, community, and divine purpose. Mixed settlements predate most modern claims of racial homelands.

**World traces:** multilingual settlements, shared sacred infrastructure, divine tools scaled for unfamiliar bodies, disputed creation sites.

## 5.3 First mortal civilizations

### Approximately 7,200–5,900 BV — The Age of Near Gods

Primearchs and Mantled Primordials walk openly in Runalis. Mortal cities form around their offices. Miracles are common but local reality increasingly reflects the resident divine lineage.

The first great caution appears: **the Verdant Communion**, a Maereth-aligned city that peacefully joins Sovael's shared organism. Later generations discover that withdrawal is nearly impossible. The resulting schism establishes continuing consent as a major theological problem.

**World traces:** enormous non-defensive cities, living civic organisms, divine-scale roads, relics designed as public utilities rather than weapons.

### Approximately 5,900–4,600 BV — The First Mortal Kingdoms

Mortal rulers seek independence from resident Primordials. Avarran office law, Varkan surrender codes, Kharumite standards, and Ilyran trade compacts permit states to function without continuous divine supervision.

Notable early powers include:

- the **Nine-Harbor Compact**, a Karakai-human-Serekin maritime confederation;
- the **Deep Measure Holds**, early dwarf cities built around fault-reading archives;
- the **Namar Continuance Houses**, custodians of memory and dangerous inheritance;
- the **Green Embassies**, elven-Myconid living border cities;
- the **Red Accord Clans**, Orc confederations whose leaders could be removed for violating surrender law.

**World traces:** layered legal tablets, multi-race architecture, crown vaults, ancestral contracts, early legendary tools.

### Approximately 4,600–3,300 BV — The Crowned Empires

Mortal states begin using Primearch recognition to legitimize expansion. Divine offices become political prizes. The greatest empires are capable, prosperous, and structurally dangerous.

- **The Auric Mandate** uses Avarran testimony systems to create reliable law, then turns universal witnessing into surveillance.
- **The Span Dominion** connects continents with Dhom Var's infrastructure, then refuses to abandon failing territories.
- **The Returning League** integrates migrants effectively but erases local claims it considers obstacles to adaptation.
- **The Veyr Continuance** safeguards lethal Orunic inheritance relics, then converts custodial duty into hereditary rule.

**World traces:** monumental roads, administrative ruins, confession instruments, sealed dynastic armories, competing layers beneath modern capitals.

## 5.4 The approach to Divine War

### Approximately 3,300–2,500 BV — The Era of Refusals

Created beings increasingly reject assigned purpose. Eidren formalizes sanctuary in the Unwritten Country. Mortal inventors produce tools that alter their own functions. Primordials demand the right to resign offices. Subjects challenge divine-backed rulers.

Many reforms improve civilization. Other refusals abandon essential systems without replacement. Crops fail when a seasonal custodian departs. A fortress refuses all entry, including refugees. Competing claims to selfhood produce legal and metaphysical crises.

**World traces:** self-altering weapons, abandoned divine offices, contradictory law codes, sanctuaries with no fixed map.

### Approximately 2,500–1,900 BV — The War of Offices

Conflicts begin over who may release a Primordial or ruler from a purpose others depend upon. Varka creates Malakar to break final sieges after several self-willed fortresses trap civilian populations.

Seraphel repeatedly overrides lawful authorities to rescue endangered populations. His popularity grows beyond Avarr's control. Nymbr and Eidren collaborate on protected private memory before becoming enemies over the stability of identity.

**World traces:** siege-breaker restraints, emergency hospitals, office-crowns without owners, fortresses split between willing and unwilling components.

### Approximately 1,900–1,200 BV — The Many Histories

Eidren opens unrealized possibilities to protect communities from inevitable destruction. The tactic succeeds locally and destabilizes chronology globally. Victorious and defeated versions of armies meet. Several peoples acquire incompatible ancestries.

Bloodline transformations become common as mortals host Primordial Essence to remain coherent across fractures. The earliest Nephilim, Cambion, and Vampire lineages date from this period.

**World traces:** duplicate ruins, people in murals who never existed in local genealogy, weapons remembering different owners, bloodline vaults.

### Approximately 1,200–0 BV — The Divine War Proper

Open war spreads through all realms. Major events include:

- **The Breaking of Seven Roads:** Concordant Ways collapse, stranding armies, refugees, and realm-machines in Runalis.
- **The Mercy Interventions:** Seraphel saves several cities by suspending civic choice under emergency protection. Most request his rule; not all are later permitted to withdraw cleanly.
- **The Siege Without End:** Malakar breaks a self-repairing fortress by consuming its boundary-oaths. The surviving population is freed, but surrounding treaties fail for generations.
- **The Quiet Rebellion:** Edrane destroys resurrection engines on both sides.
- **The Stone Survival:** Dhom Var preserves a doomed city by making its population part of an immortal bridge structure.
- **The Last Open Country:** Eidren permits every threatened future to remain possible at once, nearly collapsing historical continuity.

The Eight agree to the Veiling. Some do so from fear, some from duty, some to retain power, and some because no alternative remains.

**World traces:** the largest dungeons and legendary battlefields; Primearch prisons; realm scars; divine weapons; populations with disputed history.

## 5.5 The Veiling and ash centuries

### 0 AC — The Veiling

Eidren is cut from the Concordant order. The Unwritten Country becomes inaccessible. Refusal is dispersed through mortal selfhood. Nymbr and Veyu construct memory seals; Avarr rewrites public treaties; Kharum anchors the remaining structure; Orun protects final refusal; the other Gods stabilize routes and populations.

The Eight begin suppressing accurate Ninth knowledge because remembrance can reopen access. Later institutions suppress it because the lie legitimizes them.

**World traces:** sealed ninth chambers, altered numerals, temple renovations, Gods depicted destroying an unnamed monster, records with structurally meaningful omissions.

### 1–260 AC — The Ash Centuries

Divine routes fail. Empires lose miraculous infrastructure. Stranded Primordials either retreat, become mortal, consume Essence, or transform regions. Famine, migration, and local wars destroy much pre-war knowledge.

The Eight do not appear openly. Mortals interpret absence as judgment, victory, exhaustion, or proof that the Gods were myths.

**World traces:** abandoned utilities mistaken for temples, Primordial enclaves, collapsed road networks, scavenged divine materials.

### 260–520 AC — The First Rebuildings

New kingdoms form atop imperial remains. They inherit roads and machines without understanding original obligations. Local faiths elevate accessible Primordials as gods. Early Gravecaller and Thornbinder traditions preserve practical knowledge outside formal temples.

Orrakai's earliest permanent settlement grows around a defensible road junction, old Avarran civic foundations, and a sealed pre-Veiling threshold beneath what later becomes its religious quarter.

**World traces:** rough fortifications built into divine-scale plazas; folk shrines covering dangerous controls; hybrid craft traditions.

## 5.6 Great empires and collapses

### 520–910 AC — The Second Crown Age

Regional powers rediscover fragments of divine technology.

- **House Veyr** becomes custodian of Orunic inheritance relics and develops a durable mortuary state around Castle Veyr.
- **The Echoryn Principalities** manage forest, river, and old-road resources through contested Maereth/Avarr customs.
- **The Aureate League** restores sections of the Span roads and grows wealthy protecting pilgrimage and trade.
- Karakai route-cities reconnect coastal exchange while refusing fixed imperial borders.

Legendary weapons from this era combine ancient components with mortal repair, making them historically layered rather than pristine divine gifts.

### 910–1138 AC — The Veyr Ascendancy

House Veyr uses inherited memory, disciplined burial law, and relic custodianship to stabilize a wide region. Later rulers convert entrusted memory into political surveillance and experiment with interrupted death to preserve officeholders.

Vampire bloodlines spread through both voluntary survival pacts and coerced court rites. A civil conflict fragments the Veyr mantle. Castle Veyr remains inhabited by duties, shades, and bloodline claims after the dynasty collapses.

**World traces:** noble tomb courts, blood-sealed archives, civil-war weapons, contradictory portraits, mortuary machinery.

### 1138–1214 AC — The Three Collapses

Three systems fail within one lifetime:

1. Veyr succession wars break regional governance.
2. restored Span roads collapse after centuries of deferred maintenance.
3. a Maereth-aligned blight response consumes several farming regions while trying to correct extractive agriculture.

Orrakai expands as refugees, wardens, traders, and craftspeople fortify the old junction. Echoryn becomes a frontier of inherited ruins rather than untouched wilderness.

## 5.7 The Covenant and the Last Dawn

### 1217 AC — Founding of the Covenant of Light

The Covenant begins as an alliance of Avarran hospitals, road wardens, refugee kitchens, burial houses, and engineers responding to the Three Collapses. Its founding principle is credible: no person should be abandoned because local rulers or temples cannot cooperate.

Seraphel recognizes the Covenant after it protects civilians from its own soldiers during a quarantine dispute. This recognition gives it enormous legitimacy.

The Covenant standardizes healing, roads, records, and disaster response. It saves hundreds of thousands of lives over subsequent centuries.

**World traces:** early humble kitchens beneath later monumental chapels; practical medical tools predating purification doctrine; local memorials thanking Covenant workers.

### 1280–1460 AC — The Luminous Expansion

The Covenant becomes a transnational institution. Public testimony and standardized identity records reduce exploitation and missing-person crimes. The same systems gradually make undocumented life nearly impossible.

Purification begins as a desperate method for separating invasive Primordial Essence from afflicted people. Early practitioners record memory loss, emotional flattening, and damaged refusal as unacceptable side effects.

Later administrations reclassify those harms as evidence of successful serenity.

### 1492 AC — The First Last Dawn

A coalition of soldiers, archivists, failed-purification survivors, and frontier clergy discovers that the Covenant has sealed evidence contradicting official divine history. They form the **Order of the Last Dawn**.

The name refers to their belief that humanity is living in a long false morning: civilization sees light but has not experienced an honest dawn since the Divine War.

The early Order seeks disclosure and defensive independence. Covenant repression, internal losses, and repeated supernatural disasters radicalize later cells.

**World traces:** repurposed Covenant equipment, redacted archives recopied by hand, dawn symbols deliberately incomplete, memorials naming Order atrocities alongside Covenant disappearances.

### 1530–1690 AC — The Frontier Compacts

Orrakai formalizes its wardens, market protections, craft guilds, and uneasy Covenant relationship. It accepts medical and road assistance while resisting full religious jurisdiction.

Echoryn's old sites are reopened for mining, settlement, and salvage. Castle Veyr, the Broken Chapel, deep roads, and Red Seal structures become recurring sources of relics and disaster.

The Order develops decentralized cells. Some protect communities abandoned by the Covenant. Others steal relics, assassinate officials, and cause mass casualties to prevent divine consolidation.

## 5.8 The hidden conspiracy and manufactured hope

This section is objective late-game canon. It must not appear as ordinary historical knowledge.

### 1588 AC — Formation of the Meridian Synod

Senior Covenant figures, court scholars, relic engineers, and Seraphel-aligned mystics form a hidden organization eventually called the **Meridian Synod**. Publicly they belong to different institutions and sometimes oppose one another.

They conclude that recurring war, Rot, possession, and political collapse arise from mortal refusal—the instability left by the absent Ninth principle. They intend to recover and domesticate that principle inside a controlled human vessel.

They are distinct from the Order of the Last Dawn. The Order suspects a conspiracy but does not initially understand its nature.

### 1640–1765 AC — The Remnant Programs

The Synod recovers fragments associated with Eidren from sealed sites and the Interstice. Experiments begin with relics, prisoners, volunteers, inherited bloodlines, and eventually children selected for spiritual resilience.

Most experiments fail through death, divided identity, uncontrolled refusal, or reality distortion. Records and bodies are hidden inside legitimate Covenant disaster sites.

The Synod learns that raw Ninth Essence cannot be commanded. It must be given an identity that appears to choose its own containment.

### 1778 AC — Birth of Zephyr Light

The sole stable child of the final program survives integration with a carefully limited concentration of Eidren's remnant Essence. He is named **Zephyr Light** and raised under engineered affection, controlled suffering, curated heroism, theological instruction, and constant observation.

Zephyr is taught that humanity's love is conditional upon his perfection and that private selfhood is the source of abandonment. He eventually understands the manipulation completely.

He chooses not to free humanity from such control, but to perfect and own it.

### 1799–1830 AC — The Making of a Messiah

Zephyr performs genuine public miracles, negotiates wars, ends epidemics, and survives disasters engineered or selectively allowed by the Synod. He becomes humanity's most celebrated living hero.

His beauty, patience, and apparent compassion are real capabilities used deliberately. Privately he experiments with dependency, memory, and identity. He does not seek companionship; he seeks a world incapable of withholding itself from him.

### 1812–1837 AC — Development of the Rot Blight

The Synod develops Rot from interacting Divine War residues, damaged Maerethic growth processes, and Ninth-remnant instability. Its intended functions are:

- create crises that cross political borders;
- make Covenant aid indispensable;
- discredit uncontrolled local faiths and Primordials;
- identify resistant bloodlines and thresholds;
- prepare populations to accept Zephyr as the only universal protector;
- generate controlled suffering from which Zephyr can publicly save them.

Rot escapes controlled trials repeatedly. The Synod treats these as data.

### 1838–1846 AC — The Quiet Catastrophes

Rot outbreaks spread through frontier regions under varying names. Covenant responders save lives, often without knowledge of the cause. The Order steals records and relics, commits violent raids, and concludes that only forbidden Divine War knowledge can expose the system.

Seraphel recognizes patterns in the outbreaks. Whether he knows the full Synod truth remains deliberately restricted for later character development, but he chooses managed stability over public panic.

Malakar remains imprisoned behind combined Kharumite, Avarran, and Nymbran seals. He remembers techniques and actors that official history erased.

## 5.9 Present day

### 1847 AC — The Caravan Attack

An Orrakai-bound caravan carries an ancient artifact recovered from a newly exposed ruin. Publicly it is a dangerous but poorly understood relic. The Order of the Last Dawn believes it can open or survive the route to Malakar's prison and obtain knowledge no surviving archive contains.

The player serves as an ordinary caravan guard. The Order attacks, takes the artifact, and leaves consequences that appear to confirm its villainy. The player survives and awakens in Orrakai.

This event begins the playable story.

### Act I historical frame

Orrakai and Echoryn contain visible layers of:

1. pre-Veiling ninefold foundations;
2. Divine War seals and stranded mechanisms;
3. Veyr mortuary rule;
4. post-collapse frontier settlements;
5. Covenant relief and purification infrastructure;
6. Order sabotage and investigation;
7. modern Rot contamination.

The player initially interprets these through the Covenant's credible public history. The Order's actions appear to threaten everyone. Malakar's summoning and defeat reveal that the Order sought knowledge, not worship, and that Seraphel cannot be accepted uncritically.

### Act II historical frame

The Frontier Marches display the Covenant at its best: safe roads, prosperous farms, hospitals, disciplined patrols, pilgrimage, and restored ancient infrastructure. The region also demonstrates how emergency protection becomes permanent administration.

Seraphel's historical miracles are verifiable. Malakar's warning becomes credible through patterns of consent removed “for protection,” not through obvious villain behavior.

### Act III historical frame

Zarynth contains the Covenant's highest offices, Zephyr's public mythology, Synod archives, experiment sites, and the political beneficiaries of managed catastrophe. The player discovers the Rot's engineered origin and the absence behind the Eight.

The Ninth becomes explicit only after the player has already encountered its architectural gaps, bloodline effects, legal remnants, broken symbols, and manipulated selfhood.

## 5.11 Canonical development consequences

Future content should follow these rules:

- A ruin must belong to a historical layer and show later reuse.
- A legendary item must have makers, repairs, owners, and costs—not merely rarity.
- A bloodline reveal must connect personal power to a historical obligation or crime.
- A dungeon must expose a process, institution, or failed solution, not exist only as a monster container.
- A Primordial boss must have a manifestation method and a reason it cannot simply remain in Runalis.
- A Primearch encounter must alter civilization or divine administration.
- A faith must improve life in observable ways before its excesses become meaningful.
- An official history should contain enough truth to remain useful.
- Ninth-God evidence should begin as structural absence and only later become a name.
- Preparation should be how players convert discovery into survival: the right ward, route, rite, testimony, tool, ally, or historical understanding.

## Final foundation statement

Runalis is a world kept stable by necessary powers that do not fully trust one another. Civilization survives because people inherit their compromises, maintain their roads, bury their dead, exchange what they cannot make alone, defend boundaries, adapt to loss, preserve private dreams, and hold power answerable.

It also survives because every person retains a fragment of the God history denies.

The central mystery is not whether the forgotten Ninth was good or evil. It is whether a civilization founded on necessary erasure can learn the truth without repeating the catastrophe—and whether freedom can be defended without pretending it has no cost.
