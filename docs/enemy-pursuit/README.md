# Enemy pursuit and spacing — 2026-10-12

The production AI reproduced a home-guard failure: an already engaged enemy turned back toward its spawn after crossing its 480px home leash, even while its target was on screen. Patrols could also take priority when only `alerted` or `awakened` was set. A separate approach rule discarded overworld engagement when line of sight was interrupted or the enemy crossed the original 80px acquisition fringe.

Engaged enemies now continue pursuing within the target's screen plus a 160px buffer (five tiles). They no longer abandon a nearby target because they travelled far from home or briefly lost line of sight. Beyond that buffer they clear engagement, cancel unfinished attacks and resume their home/patrol lifecycle. Returning guards can re-engage a visible target. Unprovoked acquisition retains the existing fringe, dungeon pursuit remains persistent, and attacks still require visibility. Split-screen target selection keeps nearby valid allies available when another player escapes. Ranged combat spacing, circling and the intentional fleeing elite retain their existing behavior.

The combat coordinator now applies soft enemy body separation through a reusable local spatial grid. Actors seek their combined body radii plus 6px of room, yielding through the existing terrain collision checks. Corrective displacement scales with the simulation step and is bounded by body size. Committed windups, basic attack presentations, charges, boss recovery, bosses, roots, freeze, stagger and inactive actors stay anchored. Separation therefore reduces crowd overlap when movement is available; it does not force occupied attack poses apart or demand impossible spacing in constrained terrain.

New regression suites:

- `node tools/verify-enemy-pursuit.mjs`: seven archetypes chasing beyond their original home leash, patrol priority, three viewport sizes, horizontal/vertical offscreen escape, obscured pursuit, return-home re-engagement, nearby split-screen allies and dormant unprovoked actors. The first home-leash assertion failed against the previous implementation and passes with the fix.
- `node tools/verify-enemy-separation.mjs`: eight exactly coincident enemies, actual pursuing melee crowds, 20/30 FPS simulation steps, committed attacks/charges and controls, walls and a one-tile corridor. Both the idle pile and moving melee crowd end with zero physical overlaps in the open-floor scenarios; minimum pursuing body distance is about 29.8px for 13px-radius bodies. [Raw overlap and spacing-cost measurements](separation.json).

The local Node measurements isolate the additional spacing work for 80 and 240 actors after initial settling. They are not browser frame times or GPU measurements. The full existing regression gate also includes these suites and the offscreen-attack suite; [gate results](../performance/regressions/results.json) and [browser combat results](../enemy-combat/browser-checks.json) record the broader checks.

The full gate finished with 22 passing suites. The previously documented Echoryn `lost hungryShrine` fixture failure remains reproducible against the preserved original source. Browser combat exercised all 15 archetypes, eight bosses, eight elite identities and an 83-actor stress encounter, with no page errors or failed requests; the mixed encounter screenshot was also inspected. Enemy counts and authored art remain intact.
